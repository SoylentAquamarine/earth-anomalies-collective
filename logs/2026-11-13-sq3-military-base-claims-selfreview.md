installations" as an unresearched bootstrap candidate. `data/prior-claims-catalog.md` has no entry for it
(Canary grid, Richat, Kazakh geoglyphs and Antarctica are covered).

**Not done yet:** no source fetched. This is a first pass.

**Design and why it's non-circular:** find the best-known public claim family (Area 51 / Groom Lake; blurred or
censored imagery of sensitive sites) and fetch independent sources on what is documented, rather than assume a
"mundane" conclusion. Two separate claim variants get separate sourcing: (a) "a secret base exists that the
government denies", (b) "blurred or pixelated areas prove a cover-up".

**Prediction:** (a) resolves to "documented, acknowledged installation, not an anomaly" (the 2013 declassified
CIA history acknowledging Groom Lake). (b) resolves to a documented, mundane imagery-policy explanation
(provider or government-requested obscuring, or licensed low-resolution tiles). I may be wrong on (b); some
blurring is genuinely government-requested, which is a documented non-mundane-but-explained case.

**Boundaries:** no private individuals named or speculated about; no guidance toward visiting any site.
Catalog entries describe only what public sources state about the claim.

**Honesty precommitment:** record each source's tier (directly fetched vs search-synthesis), disclose fetch
failures, and report the result even if it contradicts the prediction.
