# SQ-2: third human-made citation — an abandoned WWII airfield mistaken for a mystery

## Precommitment self-review (written before research)

**Known:** human-made explanations has 2 of 5 categories cited so far (military/calibration
ranges, solar/wind farms); land-art, quarry/mining, and abandoned infrastructure remain
uncited. A first search for "abandoned infrastructure" surfaced a viral "secret bunker" case in
Western Australia, directly fetched and confirmed — but on inspection it turned out to be a
deliberately fabricated/uploaded fake image (an internet "backrooms" prank), not a genuine
misidentification of real abandoned infrastructure, so it was not used for this category
despite being a solid, directly-fetched source in its own right.

**Not done yet:** no genuine abandoned-infrastructure misidentification case had been
researched and confirmed.

**Design and why it's non-circular:** search again with a narrower query excluding prank/
fabricated-image cases, then directly fetch the most load-bearing source for whatever case is
found, checking it actually describes real abandoned infrastructure rather than invented
content.

**Honesty precommitment:** disclose the sourcing tier honestly (this case rests on a blog post
and a reader comment, not a scholarly or news source) rather than presenting it as more
authoritative than it is.

## Findings — a declined case, disclosed rather than silently dropped

**The Western Australia "secret bunker" case was investigated and declined for this category**:
directly fetched (AAP FactCheck), confirmed a viral Instagram video claiming a hidden
underground meeting room on a remote WA island was explained as part of "backrooms," an
internet subculture where users deliberately upload invented, unsettling images to Google Maps/
Earth as hidden "Easter eggs" — the image itself (featuring a recurring fictional character,
"Billy le Robot") was fabricated and uploaded by a contributor, then removed by Google for
policy violation. Rated **False** by AAP FactCheck. **This is a real, well-sourced case, but it
doesn't fit "abandoned infrastructure" honestly** — it's deliberately fabricated content, not a
misidentification of something real. Not added to this category to avoid mislabeling it.

**The case actually used**: a large triangular shape in the desert near Wittmann (also
described as near Surprise), Arizona, visible on Google Earth and discussed online since at
least 2006. **Directly fetched** (a 2006 Google Earth-focused blog post, `googlesightseeing.com`):
the post itself first floats an old-airfield theory (while joking it "could, of course, be
aliens!"); a 2008 commenter identifies it specifically as "Luke Auxiliary Army Airfield #4,"
citing a linked historical-airfield listing. Cross-checked against WebSearch synthesis of
several independent secondary sources (a CosmoQuest astronomy forum thread, Wikimapia, and
earth.com) that converge on the same identification: a WWII-era satellite/auxiliary training
airfield for Luke Air Force Base, inactive for decades, also historically known as "Wickenburg
Field."

## Outcome

A real, disclosed-tier citation for the "abandoned infrastructure" category: human-made
explanations now has 3 of 5 categories cited. **Sourcing tier disclosed honestly**: this rests
on a 2006 hobbyist blog post and a reader comment citing an airfield listing, not a scholarly or
major-news source — weaker than the solar/wind-farm citation's named credentialed debunkers.
Land-art and quarry/mining remain the two uncited categories.

## Limitation disclosed

The specific triangular shape's own purpose (why a triangle, specifically) is not explained by
any source found — only that it's a decommissioned airfield generally, with the triangular
runway layout itself left as an untraced detail of period airfield design, not independently
investigated this cycle.
