# SQ-2 — three more natural-formation categories cited

**Trigger:** last cycle's own named gap: three of six/seven bootstrap natural-formation categories
(fault lines, oxbow scars, agricultural patterns) remained uncited after the first citation pass. This
cycle closes that gap directly.

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known: karst/sinkholes (USGS primary tier), fairy circles (directly fetched secondary, explicitly
disclosed as debated), and salt-pan polygon cracking (cross-checked, genuinely unsettled) are cited. Not
done: fault lines, oxbow scars, agricultural patterns, and the whole human-made-explanations and
land-imagery-pipeline sections.

## Design and why it's non-circular

Researched each remaining category independently, cross-checking search results against the most
authoritative source available for each (NASA/USGS reporting for fault scarps, Britannica for fluvial
geomorphology, USGS imagery documentation plus a NASA-quoted report for center-pivot irrigation). Did not
stop at the first tidy-sounding explanation — the fault-scarp research specifically surfaced real
documented cases where a linear feature initially treated as a fault turned out not to be one, and that
caveat is kept in the catalog rather than dropped for a cleaner-sounding entry.

## Honesty precommitment

Report each category's actual sourcing tier, and keep the fault-line entry's own disclosed
unreliability-as-sole-evidence point intact rather than smoothing it into an unqualified identification
guide.

## Result

Three categories added to `data/explanation-reference.md`:

1. **Fault lines/lineaments** — a scarp is the strongest visual sign, but linear features in imagery are
   frequently non-tectonic (documented real cases: a tested Tehran-area scarp turned out to be an ancient
   shoreline; a Mobile Bay feature may be wave-cut, not fault-related). Confirmation needs fieldwork, not
   imagery alone.
2. **Oxbow lakes/meander scars** — standard fluvial geomorphology (erosion/deposition narrows a meander
   neck until a flood cuts through), Britannica-sourced. A river's scar density is itself a maturity proxy.
3. **Agricultural center-pivot/contour patterns** — center-pivot irrigation's fixed-radius sweep produces
   the characteristic circle, confirmed even for desert examples (Egyptian Sahara, NASA-quoted); gravity
   channel irrigation produces rectangles instead, and not every circular field is a pivot.

All search-synthesis tier, cross-checked against multiple sources, not single-source assertions.

## Decision

Five of six/seven original bootstrap natural-formation categories are now cited (only volcanic/impact
structures remains). The entire human-made-explanations section and land-imagery-pipeline-artifacts
section (cloud/shadow, stitching seams, 3D-mesh glitches) remain completely untouched — the clearest,
largest remaining gap in SQ-2's deliverable for a future cycle.
