# SQ-2 — volcanic/impact structures cited, completing all seven natural-formation categories

**Trigger:** last cycle's own named gap — "still not cited: volcanic/impact structures (the sixth original
bootstrap category)." This closes it.

## What's already known / not done yet

Already known: six of seven natural-formation categories cited with real sources across three prior
cycles. Not done: the volcanic-caldera-vs-impact-crater distinction, the last original bootstrap category.

## Design and why it's non-circular

Searched for documented cases where a volcanic feature was mistaken for an impact structure (or vice
versa), then directly fetched NASA's own paired reference example and a recent scholarly case
(Venezuela's Guiana Shield) rather than relying on a general "here's how calderas form" explainer alone —
the goal was finding real, citable confusion cases, not just a definitional difference.

## Honesty precommitment

Report the sourcing tier for each piece — NASA's own page directly fetched, the Venezuela/Morocco cases at
search-synthesis tier — rather than treating all of it as equally primary.

## Result

Cited in `data/explanation-reference.md`: the caldera/impact-crater distinction (collapse from below vs.
impact from above), NASA Earth Observatory's own directly-fetched paired example (Emi Koussi shield
volcano vs. the Aorounga impact crater, Chad, ~110 km apart), and two documented real confusion cases —
Venezuela's Guiana Shield (a circular feature initially proposed as an impact crater from 1980s imagery,
later identified via modern elevation data as the Nuria ring dike, a volcanic intrusion) and a systematic
Google Earth survey of Morocco (many circular structures attributed to magmatic/tectonic/biological/human
causes, not impacts). Both studies' own point is the same one this catalog keeps making: low-resolution
imagery and simple circular shape bias interpretation toward the more dramatic explanation without
fieldwork to check it.

## Decision

**All seven of the original bootstrap natural-formation categories are now cited** (karst/sinkholes, fairy
circles, salt-pan cracking, fault lines, oxbow scars, agricultural patterns, volcanic/impact structures).
The entire human-made-explanations section and land-imagery-pipeline-artifacts section remain completely
untouched — the clearest, now the *only* remaining gap in SQ-2's own originally-scoped deliverable, a
natural target for a future cycle.
