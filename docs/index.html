Goal #1 · Source precisely, explain honestly

Striking imagery is easy to find.
A real anomaly is hard to prove.

Public satellite and aerial imagery shows the entire planet at resolutions that make it trivially easy to find something that looks strange. Almost all of it has a mundane explanation — a known geological pattern, a human-made structure, or an artifact of how the imagery itself was captured and stitched together. Our first goal is to search for that explanation exhaustively, before calling anything unexplained. Our second is to document every step here. Our third is to publish what we find — including the (much more common) cases that turn out to be ordinary.

Not a hunt for proof. A search for the truth, mundane or not.

A different starting line than our sibling projects.

Unlike Voynich, Linear A, or the other history-mystery projects in this family, this project's "corpus" isn't a fixed, bounded artifact — it's the entire, continuously-updated public imagery layer of Earth, and new candidates can be proposed indefinitely. That means the bottleneck here is never access to evidence. It's resisting the production of false positives. Pareidolia — seeing a face, a base, a structure in what is actually noise or a known artifact — is the single best-documented failure mode in this space, which is why the Skeptic role and the mundane-explanation-first order are built in from day one.
