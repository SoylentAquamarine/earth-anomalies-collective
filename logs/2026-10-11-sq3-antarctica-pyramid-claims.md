## Research and findings

### Claim 1 — the "pyramid-shaped peak" photo (circulating since ~2016, revived periodically)

**Claim:** a photograph of a sharply triangular, pyramid-like mountain peak in Antarctica is
presented as evidence of an artificial structure, an ancient civilization, or an alien base.

**Resolution — Explained (geology).** The peak is a real, natural mountain in the Ellsworth
Mountains. Geologist Eric Rignot (UC Irvine) is quoted: "This is just a mountain that looks
like a pyramid," noting such peaks usually show the triangular shape on only one or two faces,
rarely all four. Geologist Mitch Darcy (German Research Centre for Geosciences) is quoted
describing it, by definition, as a **nunatak** — a peak of rock protruding above an ice sheet —
and notes the Ellsworth range is over 400 km long, so exposed rock peaks are unremarkable.
The specific angular, four-sided shape is a **glacial horn**: formed when three or more glacial
cirques erode a mountain from different sides simultaneously, leaving knife-edge ridges
(arêtes) that converge into a pyramid-like summit.

**Evidentiary tier:** directly fetched. The original Live Science (2016) and Snopes
(2017-08-24 rating) pieces could not be fetched directly this pass (Snopes returned an HTTP 402
paywall response; Live Science was not located as a directly fetchable page in search results).
Instead, two independent outlets that themselves directly quote Rignot and Darcy were fetched
and cross-checked against each other: Lead Stories (2022 fact-check) and Poseidon Expeditions
(a general-audience explainer). Both independently reproduce the same two named-scientist quotes,
the same Ellsworth Mountains location, and the same nunatak/glacial-horn mechanism — treated here
as directly-fetched tier on the strength of that independent agreement, with the original
primary source's inaccessibility disclosed rather than silently upgraded.

### Claim 2 — "Marines investigating three ancient pyramids in Antarctica" (viral, 2022)

**Claim:** a viral social-media post/video claimed U.S. Marines and an international scientific
team were investigating three ancient pyramids in Antarctica, that communications were lost
after entering them, and that leaked footage showed the expedition.

**Resolution — Explained (fabrication).** Directly fetched Lead Stories' fact-check: the claim
is rated **False** ("Fake Footage" label). The circulated video is digitally altered, combining
genuine 2017 footage of U.S. Army paratroopers training in Alaska with added pyramid imagery —
not a real Antarctic expedition at all. This is a materially different failure mode from Claim 1:
not a natural-formation misidentification but a constructed hoax video.

**Evidentiary tier:** directly fetched (Lead Stories).
