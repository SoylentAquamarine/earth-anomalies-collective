## Findings

### Human-made explanations: military/industrial test ranges and calibration targets

**Case**: large geometric white-line patterns in the Gobi Desert, northwestern China (~1 mile
long, ~3,000 feet across, with several similar nearby arrays), frequently presented online as
mysterious or unexplained.

**Directly fetched** (ABC News, 2011): two named experts offer non-mysterious explanations.
Charles Vick of GlobalSecurity.org (who monitors military/space issues) gives his best guess as
"a calibration ground facility for imaging and radar imaging systems in earth orbit." Benjamin
Radford, a skeptic, considers a military target-practice range the most likely explanation,
while noting conspiracy-minded readers will prefer a "Chinese equivalent of Area 51" framing.
Neither explanation requires anything exotic; both are ordinary military/calibration
infrastructure.

**Search-synthesis tier only, not independently re-fetched this pass**: a related, separately-
documented case — Google Earth's 2008-2016 imagery gap over the Tonopah Test Range (Nevada,
Department of Energy/Air Force) — is a second, well-known real-world example of the same
general "test range reads as a mysterious gap/pattern" pattern, named here for a future direct-
fetch pass, not relied upon at the same tier as the China case above.

### Land-imagery-pipeline artifacts: multi-provider stitching seams

**Case**: a 2021 viral claim that Google Earth was "blocking" a section of the Suez Canal, based
on that section appearing in a visibly darker blue than the surrounding water.

**Directly fetched** (PolitiFact, rated the claim **False**): Google's own satellite-imagery
expert, Matt Manolides, is quoted directly: imagery is compiled from many different providers
(state agencies, geological survey organizations, commercial providers), "taken on different
dates and under different lighting and weather conditions" — producing visible seams when tiles
from different sources/dates are mosaicked together. Google separately notes that stitching
artifacts are often more visible over water specifically. PolitiFact independently found the
same visual effect at three other unrelated locations (the Panama Canal, the Straits of
Gibraltar, Loch Ness) — directly supporting that this is a general pipeline artifact, not a
location-specific anomaly or deliberate censorship.
